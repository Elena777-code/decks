A · a passive to report

Say it with a passive

we report an action —be + participle— and the reporter stays unnamed
People in the area have been told that they should stay indoors.
Everyone was asked to bring some food to the party.

Use a passive to report what people say, think, etc. — particularly when it is not important to mention who is being reported.

Tap a sentence to hear it 🔊

B · it + passive verb + that-clause

It is said that…

It+passive verb+that+ clause — the news goes to the END
It is reported that the damage is extensive.compare: The damage is extensive, according to government sources.
It was decided that the meeting should be cancelled.or: It was decided to cancel the meeting.
also:allegeannounceassumebelievecalculateclaimconsiderdemonstratediscoverestablishestimateexpectfeelfindknowmentionrecommendrevealsayshowsuggestsupposethinkunderstand
these also take a to-infinitive clause:agreedecidehopeintendplanpropose

B · the verbs that refuse it

Not every verb takes it

✓ We have been informed that we have to leave.✗ It has informed us that…
also (pattern A, never it):encouragepersuadereassureremindtellwarn

These verbs need a personal object before the that-clause in an active form — They have informed us that…

Tap the sentence to hear it 🔊

C · subject + passive verb + to-infinitive

Put the subject first

the subject becomes the topic:subject+passive verb+to-infinitive
It is reported that the damage is extensive.
⇄ and
The damage is reported to be extensive.
not in this pattern:announcedecidementionproposerecommendsuggest
✓ I was told (= ordered) to go with them to the railway station.✗ The accident was told to have happened just after midnight.

We can only use tell in this pattern when it means order.

Tap a sentence to hear it 🔊

D · it + passive verb + wh-clause

Report what was found out

It+passive verb+who · whether · how …
It has now been revealed who was responsible for the accident.
The decision to build the bridge was taken before it was established whether it was actually needed.
also:discoverexplainfindknowrevealshowunderstand

Tap a sentence to hear it 🔊

E · there + passive verb + to be

There is said to be…

that + there …→there + passive + to be / to have been
It is thought (that) there are / were too many obstacles to peace.
⇄ and
There are thought to be / to have been too many obstacles to peace.

to be = present · to have been = past. The same verbs as pattern C.

Headlines → it-passives 📰

1 · AGREEMENT THAT UN WILL SEND IN TROOPS

exercises · 25.3 (C)

The interview 🎙

Write two new sentences for each: it + passive verb + that-clause in one, subject + passive verb + to-infinitive in the other. (The second pattern may not always be possible.)

(1) We have discovered that a mechanical fault caused the problem. (2) We don't think that the fault is serious. (3) We expect that it will take several weeks to correct the fault. (4) We have decided to postpone the next rocket launch, and (5) we suggest that the next launch should take place in May.
1It has been discovered that a mechanical fault caused the problem. / A mechanical fault was discovered to have caused the problem.
2It is not thought that the fault is serious. (or It is thought that the fault is not serious.) / The fault is not thought to be serious.
3It is expected that it will take several weeks to correct the fault. (or …that the fault will take several weeks to correct.) / The fault is expected to take several weeks to correct.
4It has been decided to postpone the next rocket launch.no second pattern — decide is a pattern-C exception
5It is suggested that the next launch should take place in May.no second pattern — suggest is a pattern-C exception
