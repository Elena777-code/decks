The picture · drawn by the teacher

One trip home. One trip away.

been — She has been to the palm tree… and look, she’s back at the house.
gone — She has gone to the palm tree… and she’s still there. The house is empty.

Let HER read the picture first: two arrows, two destinations. Ask: «Which person is at home now?» (the been one). Ask: «Can you visit the gone person at home?» (no — she’s away). The sketch is Elena's own — the hand-drawn labels stay.
The rule · two words, two places

Been = round trip. Gone = one way.

has been — went, and came back

🏠The person is here now.
✈️«She has been to Turkey.» — the holiday is over, the photos are on the phone.
🔁Ask it about life experience: «Have you ever been to London?»

has gone — went, and is still there

🌴The person is NOT here.
🚪«Mum has gone to the shop.» — the house is quiet.
📍You say it about now, when the empty chair matters.

🧠 Two true lines: both words are purple — both fly with has/have. Only one puts the person back at home. Which one? → been — the round trip. The house doors open for been, and stay empty for gone.

Teach step — the two cards say it all; the colours: both are present perfect (purple, the standing code) — the DIFFERENCE is geography, not grammar. The hook line: been = round trip, gone = one way. Say the hook twice.
Your turn · been or gone?

Fill the gap — say it before you click

1Mum isn’t here — she ______ to the shop. → has gone (the house is empty)
2I ______ to London twice. → have been (two round trips)
3Where’s Dan? — He ______ to the football match, it’s on now. → has gone (the match is now)
4She’s back from her holiday — she ______ to Turkey. → has been (she’s home)
5My parents ______ to Italy — they come home on Sunday. → have gone (still there)
6Have you ever ______ to New York? → been (life experience)
7Ed isn’t at college today — he ______ home for the weekend. → has gone (away till Sunday)
8Look, she’s making tea in the kitchen — she ______ to the beach. → has been (she’s back — the tea proves it)

The palm-tree test: can she be at home right now? Yes → been. No → gone.

Answers hide — she says the word, then clicks. The palm-tree test is the one-line crutch: at home right now = been; away = gone. Item 8 is the tricky one (tea = home = been even though the beach was the destination).
Treasure task · read the clues

Where is Max?

Max’s bike is not in the yard. The front door is locked from the outside, and his mum is at work.

Question: Max has been to the skate park — or Max has gone to the skate park? → has GONE — nobody is home: the door locked from outside says the trip is one way, for now

Two clues, one question — the empty house picks the word.

THE TREASURE TASK (standing): two true statements + one question whose answer follows — and the answer is NOT in the statements (the deck-010 lesson: the answer on the screen = «I felt stupid»). Derivation: locked from outside + mum at work ⇒ the house is empty ⇒ Max is still out ⇒ has gone. She says it aloud before the click.
One line to remember

Been = round trip. Gone = one way.

🏠been — the person is home. Ask about life: Have you ever been to…?
🌴gone — the person is away. Say about now: Mum has gone to the shop.
