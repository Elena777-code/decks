Recap · 8.4 have/get something done — for anyone who missed last lesson

Quick recap before we move on

Board: have some photos taken · have your hair cut · have the costumes made · have the poster printed · have your face painted — elicit the pattern: have + object + past participle = someone else does it for you.

From 8.4 Ex 4 — complete with the correct have form + the verb in brackets (whole class, board or mini-whiteboards):

  • 1 A burglar made a mess... we're going to have it cleaned (clean).
  • 2 We need to catch the robber. Let's ___ posters ___ (print) with his photo.
  • 3 The detective always ___ a coffee ___ (bring) to him when he arrives.
  • 4 That girl chased the criminal, then she ___ her photo ___ (take) for the newspaper.
  • 5 I never leave my bike without a lock. I don't want to ___ it ___ (steal).
  • 6 He writes great crime stories. He should ___ them ___ (make) into films.

Answers: 2 have, printed · 3 has, brought · 4 had, taken · 5 have, stolen · 6 have, made

8.5 · Lead-in — review of have/get something done

Problem → advice

Tell ss to think of a problem someone needs to fix for them, e.g. "I can't see the board very well." "My phone won't connect to the internet."

Remind them how to give advice/suggestions: You should…, Why don't you…?, If I were you, I would…

Mingle — different partners take turns to say their problem and give advice using have/get something done, e.g. "I can't see the board very well." — "If I were you, I would have your eyes tested."

Ex 5 · WORD FRIENDS

arrest a criminal · interview a witness · look for clues · search the area · solve a crime · take fingerprints

Choose the correct option, using a dictionary if needed:

  • 1 Detectives can solve a crime / take fingerprints more quickly when there's a good witness.
  • 2 Police want to interview a witness / arrest a criminal who saw somebody go into the house.
  • 3 We believe the police are going to search the area / arrest a criminal this evening, but they won't say who it is.
  • 4 The crime took place near the forest, and police officers are solving a crime / searching the area now.
  • 5 The police couldn't take fingerprints / interview a witness because the burglar was wearing gloves.
  • 6 The detective is arresting a criminal / looking for clues at the crime scene, but she hasn't found anything yet.

Answers: 2 interview a witness · 3 arrest a criminal · 4 searching the area · 5 take fingerprints · 6 looking for clues

After checking, discuss each phrase and elicit other useful word friends, e.g. find clues, leave fingerprints.

Extra activity

Dictation → Past Simple Passive

Dictate these sentences; ss rewrite them in the Past Simple Passive on paper, then swap to check — a point for each correct sentence.

1 The police arrested the criminal. → The criminal was arrested by the police. 2 The police interviewed the witness. → The witness was interviewed by the police. 3 The police found some clues. → Some clues were found by the police. 4 The police searched the area. → The area was searched by the police.

Ex 7 · speaking

What would you do?

Before ss answer, discuss which structure to use and why: the Second Conditional, because the situations are unlikely. Elicit the full question/answer for one, e.g. "What would you do if you had your mobile phone stolen in town?" — "If I had my mobile phone stolen in town, I would…"

In pairs: 1) You have your mobile phone stolen in town. 2) You see a person who is shoplifting. 3) Some people are vandalising a wall near your school.
