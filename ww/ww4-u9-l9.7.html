Dear Nadia,

Thanks for your letter. I’m looking forward to meeting you as well.

You asked about my school. Well, there are about a thousand pupils, and the teachers are friendly. We wear uniforms, but I can lend you a spare one if you like. I have basketball club after school on Fridays, so you’re welcome to come along.

Kyla

Vocabulary · SB ex. 4 (p. 113) · the phrases in colour

Five phrases from the letter

What do they mean? Why does Kyla use them? Click each one:

1I’m looking forward to meeting you → she’s excited about it — a friendly letter ending: I’m looking forward to hearing more about…
2…a spare one, if you like → “if that’s what you want” — often with an offer: I can help you if you like.
3you’re welcome to come along → an invitation to something already arranged: I’m going to the cinema — you’re welcome to come along, too.
4here’s a photo, too, just in case → to prevent problems: take an umbrella just in case it rains.
5Don’t worry about anything → everything is organised — it reassures someone doing something new: the airport, the family, the school — all ready.

Name the tenses

First — from your life: a past event, a habit, a plan, a promise. Then — Kyla’s letter: match each tense with its job. Click to check:

1something happening now → Present Continuous — I’m looking forward
2a past event → Past Simple — You asked about my school
3regular habits (and facts) → Present Simple — there are · wear · I have basketball club
4plans → be going to — we’re going to meet · going to have fun
5a promise → will — I’ll be at the airport

Writing · SB ex. 6 (p. 113) · the Writing box

A letter has six stages

Complete gaps a–d with sentences from Kyla’s letter:

Writing · A letter giving information
1Starting your letter — Dear (name),… · It was good to hear from you. → a
2Making it clear why you’re writing — You asked for information about the school here. → b
3Giving useful information — There are about a thousand pupils. · I can lend you a uniform / PE kit / bag. → c
4Making arrangements — We’ll definitely meet you. · We’ll be there at 7.15 p.m. → d
5Before you finish — I’m really looking forward to seeing you (again). · We’re going to have a great time.
6Ending your letter — All the best, / See you soon,
a→ Thanks for your letter.
b→ You asked about my school.
c→ I have basketball club after school on Fridays.
d→ …so you’re welcome to come along.

Books closed! — How did she start? Why was she writing? What information? What arrangements? How did she finish?
