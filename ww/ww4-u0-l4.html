GRAMMAR · Present Perfect

Affirmative

+He has (just/already) directed a film.  ·  They have moved to England.

Negative

−I haven’t (ever)/I have never lived in the US.  ·  He hasn’t taken part in a marathon (yet).

Questions

?Have you (ever) visited the US? — Yes, I have./No, I haven’t.  ·  Has she (ever) run a 5K race? — Yes, she has./No, she hasn’t.

For and since

⏱They’ve lived in England for a year. (a period of time)  ·  She’s been here since April. (a point in time)

In the text: → have been here (for a year now) · hasn’t been here (for so long) · he’s discovered … and met … · Abe has done a lot … · He’s taken some awesome photos · he’s taken up a new hobby: film-making · He’s already directed a film · He’s also run a 5K race · he hasn’t taken part in any longer races yet · he’s spent a lot of time hanging out … · he’s made some great new friends · They’ve had a lot of fun together

Worksheet · Rule Present Perfect

The rule — complete it with “move”

Stick the worksheet in your copybook. Complete the rule with the correct form of the verb move. Then explain the form.

+I / you / we / they ________ (already/just) ________→ have moved to England.
+He / she / it ________ (already/just) ________→ has moved to England.
−I / you / we / they ________ (ever) ________ (yet)→ haven’t moved to England.
−He / she / it ________ (ever) ________ (yet)→ hasn’t moved to England.
?________→ Have I / you / we / they (ever) ________→ moved to England?
?________→ Has he / she / it (ever) ________→ moved to England?

The Present Perfect = have/has + the 3rd form (the past participle). move → moved (regular).
