Part D · Reading · first the text

The junior firefighter

Read once, calmly. The questions are on the next page.

When Jake was sixteen, he joined the junior firefighter program in his small American town. The juniors train every Saturday morning: they learn to use the equipment, carry the hoses and help the senior firefighters at real emergencies. They are not allowed to enter burning buildings — that is the rule — but they do everything else.

Last month Jake's team was called to a fire in an old barn. While the seniors fought the flames, Jake and two other juniors carried equipment, brought water and helped the family's cat out of a tree (yes, it really happens). Nobody was hurt.

"People think we just wash the trucks," Jake says. "But last year the juniors of our station gave more than 2,000 hours. We are not heroes. We just love our town and want to be useful."

Jake wants to become a professional firefighter after school, like his uncle. "The first thing they teach you," he laughs, "is that a good firefighter is never in a hurry. You win by staying calm."
