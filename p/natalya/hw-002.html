check yourself

check yourself

check yourself

check yourself

check yourself
→ Somebody has… somebody · everybody · nobody — always singular: has, was, does.

🧩 The grammar that got away — three points, taught.

The three sentences from yesterday’s judge game — the ones that got away. Judge each, write your corrected version, and only then open the check and the explanation. Same eight traps, a new order — whenever we play again.

① Not only… — and the words change seats

From yesterday’s judge game: “Not only she passed the exam, but she also topped the class.”

✗ Fix it — write the corrected sentence:

check + explanation
→ “Not only did she pass the exam, but she also topped the class.”
Why: when not only starts the sentence, the words change seats — the question order follows: did she pass, never she passed. The verb jumps over the subject.

② It is the brain that… — never “what”

From yesterday’s judge game: “It is the brain what makes teenagers take risks.”

✗ Fix it — write the corrected sentence:

check + explanation
→ “It is the brain that makes teenagers take risks.”
Why: the spotlight construction (the cleft) takes that or who — never what. The spotlight shines on the brain; that connects it to the verb.

③ I'd rather she didn't… — the polite unreal past

From yesterday’s judge game: “I’d rather she doesn’t take her phone to bed.”

✗ Fix it — write the corrected sentence:

check + explanation
→ “I'd rather she didn't take her phone to bed.”
Why: would rather + another person → the past form, unreal and polite: didn't take — no present, no -s. You're not reporting; you're wishing.

💬 “How are you?” — four answers, four weathers.

One small thing that got away — let's make it stay.

I'm fine — neutral, always safe, any room.
I'm good — relaxed, everyday, for friends.
I'm well — “I'm not ill any more” — the health answer, the one we discussed. After an illness, at the doctor's, on the phone with family: “I'm well now, thank you.” The little now carries the whole recovery. (And in greetings it grows a very: “I'm very well, thank you — and you?”)
