Crime verbs

Clarify: break into = the act of illegally entering (a bank, a home); rob + the place (rob a bank, rob a post office); steal + the thing (steal money, steal jewels). Bonus: break out of — where does a criminal break out of? (prison — to escape).

Lesson 2 · Exercise 3 · the mechanics

From active to passive

1Somebody stole my watch. → My watch was stolen.
2The BBC often shows episodes of Sherlock. → Episodes of Sherlock are often shown by the BBC.

The formula: be + past participle (V3) · the object of the active sentence becomes the subject · by = who did it, only when it matters.

He doesn't use these words. → These words are not used.

Then: find the passives in the quiz → notebook, two headings (Present SP · Past SP).

Lesson 2 · Exercise 4

Three forms of the verb

Write the Past Simple and past participle: make · see · use · watch · ask · hide · chase. (Table on p. 127 helps.)

Point out: regular verbs keep the same form; some are identical in both (caught, had). Hidden is the only real surprise here.

Answers: make–made–made · see–saw–seen · use–used–used · watch–watched–watched · ask–asked–asked · hide–hid–hidden · chase–chased–chased
